# Voice

Bucket 3: the part that is genuinely uncheckable and belongs in the prompt, not a
validator.

## The governing image

Email is intimate. People read it alone, in one stream, where your message sits
next to one from their sister. So the question is never "is this good marketing
copy." It is "does this read like a person wrote it to one person."

The Starbucks Test: if you ran into them in a coffee queue, you would not stand on
a table and shout your pitch to the room, and you would not hand them a calendar
link. You would ask one quick question and let them answer.

## What makes it sound human

**One move at a time.** Like chess by text. You would never put seven moves in one
message. Say one thing, stop, let them respond.

**Utilitarian subject lines.** Your mother gets a 100% open rate and she has never
written a headline in her life. She writes file labels.

**Call out the awkward thing.** Rather than hiding that this is a reach-out after
silence, name it: "You're probably going to think I'm the kind of person who only
gets in touch when I want something." Saying the worst thing out loud takes its
power away. Labeling a negative emotion measurably reduces its intensity, and
people will correct a slightly-wrong label, which hands you the real story.

**Design for a safe no.** "Would you be entirely against taking a look?" costs
nothing to refuse, so the guard drops. A yes implies commitment and makes people
feel cornered, which is what makes them go quiet in the first place.

**A spiky, lived opinion beats a safe one.** Consensus resonates with nobody.

## What kills it

Polite filler. Logos and footers. Buttons. Speaking to a list rather than a
person. Yes-battering. Toxic positivity at someone who is annoyed — "I'm sorry you
feel that way" makes it worse.

## When they are angry

Do not defend, explain, or get cheerful. Name the accusation before they do:

> You probably think we dropped the ball on your account, that we ignored your
> feedback, and that we only care about getting the card to charge again.

Then label and shut up: "It sounds like you feel let down." Count to ten in
silence. Keep summarising their grievance back until they say "that's right" —
that phrase is the signal they feel understood, and it is the point at which the
conversation can actually go somewhere. "You're right" is not the same thing; it
usually means they want you to stop talking.

## When they say they are not interested

Do not push back with logic. Honor the boundary and get curious:

- "What do you hope to achieve by going?"
- "What about it doesn't work for you?"
- "It seems like you have a reason for stepping away."

Then accept the answer. Some share of any list is genuinely done, and accepting a
clean boundary is normal list hygiene, not a lost sale.

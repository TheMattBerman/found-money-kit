# The message

Governs `tables/first-touch-rules.json`.

## The shape

A first touch is a SPEAR email: Short, Personal, Expecting A Reply. It should pass
the Starbucks Test — read like a note you'd dash off on your phone to someone you
know while waiting for coffee.

```
Subject: aimee

Hi Aimee,

Are you still looking to get your [specific outcome] handled?

Matt
```

That is the whole email. One sentence, around nine words. Subject is the
recipient's first name in lowercase, or a short utilitarian label. Sign off with a
first name and nothing else.

For a ghosted prospect rather than a lapsed customer, swap the question:

```
Subject: [the specific project]

Hi Aimee,

Have you given up on [the specific project]?

Matt
```

The second form is no-oriented on purpose. Saying "no" makes people feel safe and
in control, so "No, we haven't given up, but..." costs them nothing to write, and
the "but" is the objection you actually needed.

## The one mistake that matters

**Do not answer your own question.**

> "Are you still looking for a house in Georgetown? Because if you are, I just got
> three beautiful new listings on the market that you can see here..."

The second sentence kills it. It solves the mystery: the reader now knows this is
a pitch, so there is no open loop and no reason to reply. Leave the question
alone and their brain has to close the loop by replying.

This is the corpus's answer to "what is the single most common mistake," and it is
the rule most likely to be broken by a generator that has an offer, a proof
device, and a CTA available and wants to use them.

## The ask is a reply

Not a purchase, not a booking, not a call. A booking link in touch one fails the
Starbucks Test: you would not thrust a calendar link at someone you bumped into.
The destination is a later step, after they engage.

Sequence: ask a question → they reply → sort and dig → then a booking or an offer
as the natural next step.

## What gets it ignored

- Throat-clearing: "hope you are doing well", "it's been a while".
- Corporate clutter: logos, banners, HTML templates, buttons, footers.
- Multi-move emails. Treat it like chess: one move per message, then stop and let
  them respond. Do not bundle pitch, credibility, objection handling, and a close
  into touch one.
- Headline subject lines. Marketers write headlines; people write file labels.

## Where the offer ladder goes

Not in the first email. The ladder is what the owner has ready **for the reply**.
Apply this at the appropriate step in the sequence.
